## 5. The three findings that should drive the next build

1. **Reranking is where the passage gap lives.** ~67% of failures are answer-bearing chunks already
   at ranks 2–10; 19% never enter the pool; 15% are vision items no text retriever can serve. The
   text arm's own R@10 (0.8167) bounds any reorder-only fix.
2. **Fusion EVICTS.** On the full corpus, dense-only puts the answer in the top-10 for 50/60
   questions; fused manages 43. Seven answers leave the pool entirely — no reranker recovers those.
3. **The vision arm is the VLM case, stated cleanly.** All 4 vision items retrieve their gold paper
   at **rank 1** and miss the gold block **everywhere** in the top-10. Retrieval reaches the right
   document every time and cannot reach the figure. A VLM reading the retrieved page would.

**Guardrail:** block-P@1 is **fixture-conditioned** (0.400 vs 0.750 on the same system, proven by a
shared-paper control). Validate any fix against the *other* fixture as a held-out control before
believing it.

## 6. Operational facts that will bite you

- **The main checkout is shared with other sessions** and is often on someone else's branch. Do not
  `git checkout` there. Use `git worktree add` and push from the worktree. A commit of mine landed
  on the wrong branch once by ignoring this.
- **ox-alpha 429s on roughly 1 request in 3** (`upstream_provider_shared_pool`). Dispatches die
  silently — exit 0, no error, log ending at `llm runtime selected`. **Assume every dispatch dies.**
- **"Commit as you go" in a brief does not produce commits.** What works: define the ticket AS a
  numbered list of commits, with commit 1 being a stub committed before any real work. Two tickets
  died with nothing under the old phrasing and succeeded under the new one.
- **`--collection waymo_av_safety` must be passed explicitly.** The default is `papers`, a
  *different corpus*, and omitting it produces plausible, meaningless numbers silently.
